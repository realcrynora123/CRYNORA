Our technology

Two ways to dry. One better fruit.

Freeze drying gives the best quality but uses a lot of energy and time. Heat pump drying is efficient but can only go so far on its own. We use both, in order.

Stage one

Heat pump drying

Air is warmed and dried in a closed loop. The heat pump cools the moist air to condense out the water, then reuses that heat to warm the air again. The fruit never meets the high temperatures of a conventional hot-air dryer, and very little energy is wasted.

Typical range: 30 to 50 °C, low humidity air

Stage two

Freeze drying

The fruit is frozen, then placed under deep vacuum. Remaining water goes from ice straight to vapour, a step called sublimation. Because it never turns to liquid, the cell structure holds and the piece stays light and crisp.

Typical range: below −30 °C, under vacuum

The process

From ripe fruit to sealed pouch.

  1. Select and slice

    Ripe fruit is washed, peeled where needed and cut to an even thickness so every piece dries at the same rate.

  2. Heat pump pre-drying

    Most of the water is removed in warm, dry air at low temperature.

  3. Freezing

    Pre-dried pieces are frozen solid, fixing their shape.

  4. Freeze drying

    Vacuum sublimation removes the last of the water, leaving a light and crisp piece.

  5. Sealed fresh

    Pouches are sealed straight away so moisture cannot get back in.

Eat well

Good food is a daily habit.

Small, steady choices add up. Here is why healthy eating matters, how much fruit to aim for, and where a pouch of Crynora fits in.

Why it matters

What a healthy diet does for you.

Steady energy

Fibre slows how quickly your body absorbs carbohydrates, so meals built on whole foods keep you going longer than sugary snacks.

A happier gut

Fibre from fruit, vegetables, grains and pulses supports regular digestion and helps you feel full for longer.

Vitamins and antioxidants

Fruit supplies vitamin C and carotenoids. Vitamin C helps you absorb iron from plant foods, and antioxidants help counter everyday cell damage.

Protection for the long run

The WHO lists low fruit and vegetable intake among the leading risk factors for death worldwide. Eating more of them is part of lowering the burden of chronic disease.

How much

Daily fruit and vegetable targets.

400 gThe WHO's daily target for fruit and vegetables together, roughly five portions.
300 g + 100 gIndia's NIN dietary guidelines suggest at least 300 g of vegetables and about 100 g of fresh fruit a day.

These targets are for fresh produce. Dried fruit is concentrated, so treat a 50 g Crynora pouch as a snack that sits alongside fresh fruit and vegetables, not in place of them.

Build a balanced plate

Half fruit and vegetables.

  • Vegetables and fruit, about halfMix colours and include leafy greens. Fresh fruit comes first, dried fruit is a bonus.
  • Whole grains, about a quarterRoti, brown rice, oats and millets.
  • Protein, about a quarterDal, beans, paneer, eggs, fish or chicken.

Smart snacking

Five habits for better snacks.

  • Read the ingredient list.Ingredients are listed by quantity, so a short list is a good sign.
  • Look for added sugar.Sugar, jaggery, honey and glucose syrup added in processing bring calories without nutrients.
  • Choose whole foods.Ultra-processed snacks lose much of their fibre and micronutrients, so keep them occasional.
  • Mind the portion.Dried fruit is concentrated, so a small handful goes a long way.
  • Pair it up.Add curd, nuts or seeds to a fruit snack to feel full for longer.
